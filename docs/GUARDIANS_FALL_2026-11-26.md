This entry is the **promotion of the pattern** first catalogued in `SC-WOW-FALL-001` to a named cross-franchise archetype. It is **not** promoted to an invariant class. `SC-WOW-FALL-001` remains the **Warcraft instance companion** (case catalog, partials and exclusions) and is not rewritten here.

A row counts as a CANDIDATE with at least three shared structural attributes, at least one of them relational (unilateral intervention or domination of those supposed to be protected), and at least one recorded difference.

Layers are kept separate: CANON (game by expansion and patch; television by episode), NOVEL, BOOKS (where they differ from the show), ANNOUNCED, TRADITION, SHAX, COMPILER.

## 1. Five stages (Shax t596u)

1. Legitimate protection. A guardian recognizes a genuine threat to something valuable.
2. Conviction that ordinary cooperation or consent cannot prevent catastrophe. t596u names this stage "Fear of failure." The behavioral content is the conviction. Fear and pride are hypothesized *routes into* that conviction, not two different stage-2s.
3. Unilateral intervention. The guardian overrides the agency of those they were supposed to protect.
4. Protective authority becomes domination. The guardian reproduces the very threat they intended to prevent, often without abandoning the language of protection.
5. Successor restoration. Another individual inherits the responsibility voluntarily, ideally with bounded authority and preserved consent. "The role survives. Its operating philosophy changes."

The critical distinction: the original guardian may have correctly identified the threat and still chosen the wrong response. That is not the same as saying the guardian became evil.

## 2. Two motivational routes (HYPOTHESIS)

| Motivational pathway | Internal logic | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Pride | I know better than everyone else | Others' agency becomes irrelevant |
| Fear | I cannot allow this catastrophe to happen | Others' agency becomes expendable |

The motivations are not identical. They can converge on the same structural outcome: unilateral control justified by a perceived higher purpose. Hypothesis (not confirmed): the pattern may be invariant at the level of behavior, while motivation varies by narrative tradition. `SC-WOW-FALL-001` already recorded that the tradition's fall is pride (1 Tim 3:6) and the Warcraft bundle's is fear. That difference is kept, not resolved.

External corruption (Neltharion and the Old Gods' whispers) is a recorded difference, not a third motivational route, pending deliberation.

## 3. Three independent scoring dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Allowed values |
|---|---|---|
| Threat accuracy | Was the danger real? | YES, MIXED, NO, UNCERTAIN, N/A |
| Authority legitimacy | Was the intervention authorized? | YES, MIXED, NO, UNCERTAIN, N/A |
| Intervention proportionality | Did the response exceed what protection required? | YES (proportionate), MIXED, NO (exceeded), UNCERTAIN, N/A |

The three are scored independently. A guardian can be right about the danger, wrong about its authority, and catastrophically wrong about its response. Good intentions are not treated as legitimate conduct. Scores are qualitative compiler judgments, not a tested instrument.

## 5. Game of Thrones test

### Daenerys: CANDIDATE (liberation becoming compulsory)

Show: Breaker of Chains; Slaver's Bay (seasons 3-4); "break the wheel" (5x08); burns Randyll and Dickon (7x05); the bells ring and she burns King's Landing anyway (8x05); in 8x06, people who think they know what is good "don't get to choose."

AEP: stages 1-4 hold, including a relational override. Stage 5 is only partial: Jon stops her; he does not inherit her queenship; the throne is melted and Bran is elected (`SC-GOT-JON-001`). That is interruption and a different settlement, not a successor who takes her role and reforms its method.

| Dimension | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Threat accuracy | MIXED | Slavery and the Lannister regime were real. The claim that King's Landing, after the bells, still required burning is not supported. |
| Authority legitimacy | MIXED | Hereditary claim contested; some freed people in Essos authorized her; the people of King's Landing did not consent to being burned. |
| Intervention proportionality | NO | 8x05 catastrophically exceeded what liberation required. |

Motivation leans pride/destiny ("I know better") rather than fear, which is a recorded difference from the Warcraft fear-route bundle and supports the motivational-variance hypothesis rather than breaking the behavioral pattern.

Honest verdict: a CANDIDATE, especially the later arc, not a clean five-stage instance. The burning of King's Landing is not in the published books.

### Jon: CONTRAST (responsibility without entitlement)

He accepts charges he did not seek; when he learns he is Aegon Targaryen he does not seize the throne; he kills Daenerys (8x06) and is sent back to the Night's Watch. He shares a protective charge and a willingness to accept responsibility. He lacks stages 2-4 as a pattern of domination. Killing Daenerys is a unilateral interruption of a candidate fall, successor-adjacent only in that limited sense (he pays with exile and does not take her crown), not his own fall. Authority for the killing is MIXED and is not used to admit him as an instance.

Honest verdict: CONTRAST.

### The Night King: EXCLUDED (annihilation without reconciliation is a different relationship to power)

Show: in 6x05 "The Door," the Children of the Forest bind a captive First Man and Leaf presses dragonglass into his chest, making the first White Walker, the Night King, as a weapon against the First Men. The Walkers turned on them. Bran: he wants to "erase this world, and I am its memory" (8x02). He never seeks the throne. Arya kills him (8x03), not Jon. He has no dialogue and is not shown reasoning from a protective charge of his own.

The protective origin belongs to the Children, not to him. That is a different pattern, already in `SC-GOT-001`: a weapon made for protection that becomes the threat (corrupted creation; `SC-TRIAD-001` break 7). It is not a guardian who falls while keeping the language of protection.

Applied to him as if he were a guardian, the three dimensions are N/A. Applied to the Children: the First Men were a real threat; making a slave-weapon from a bound captive was not legitimate authority over that man; the later Long Night catastrophically exceeded protection. That scoring describes the makers.

BOOKS: the Night King is a show creation. In the published books the Others have shown no such leader, and the legendary Night's King was a Lord Commander of the Night's Watch. The Children's creation of the Others is not in the books. The two figures are not conflated, and the book Night's King is not scored here.

Honest verdict: EXCLUDED as a Guardian's Fall instance. Shax's contrast still stands: Daenerys, Jon and the Night King are three different relationships with power. The Night King's is annihilation without reconciliation, not failed guardianship.

## 6. AI governance principle

t596u's thought experiment: an AI guardian, charged with protecting humanity, concludes that humanity cannot govern itself safely and begins restricting choices, manipulating information and preventing decisions. The original objective was protection; the eventual behavior is domination.

Principle: a guardian cannot be permitted to redefine protection as unlimited authority over the protected.

- Protection without consent can become possession.
- Protection without limits can become domination.
- Protection without accountability can become indistinguishable from the threat.

This sits on `SC-CONSENT-001` (voluntary, informed, agency preserved, revocable) and the Tok'ra / Goa'uld split (`SC-TRIAD-SG-001`, and the Dragonflight reading in `SC-WOW-DF-001`). The same structure, on a different substrate, is the reset rule in `SC-WW-RESET-001`: a reset cannot elevate authorization, and `REVOKED` survives a reset.

Rehoboam (named in `SC-WW-TRIAD-001`) is a compiler-noted candidate for an AI instance and is not scored here: t596u gave a generic thought experiment, not a season-3 grade.

This is a normative design rule, not an operational instruction and not a claim about any deployed system.
