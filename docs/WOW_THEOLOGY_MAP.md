# World of Warcraft through theology (2026-11-25m)

Source: Shax, t594u, verbatim in `docs/evidence/source-2026-11-25m/t594u_message_body.txt` (sha256 in that folder's README). In his words: "map out all the invariants and classes of World of Warcraft and bosses throughout theology", with Dragonflight framed as "the good Tok'ra versus the bad ghouls" ('ghouls' is his word for the Goa'uld, kept as written).

Method: SC-AEP-001. We look for structure, not one-to-one matches. A resemblance is a **candidate correspondence, not proof**. Three layers stay apart throughout:

- **CANON**: the game, by expansion and patch, through the Warcraft Wiki (warcraft.wiki.gg) and Blizzard News, retrieved 2026-11-25. Novel-only material is marked NOVEL, and unreleased content is marked ANNOUNCED.
- **TRADITION**: scripture cited by verse, using KJV wording where quoted. Interpretive tradition is labelled as interpretation.
- **SHAX_INTERPRETATION**: his framing, kept as his. It is not canon and it is not tradition.

Compiled entries (all SILVER by rule, none GOLD): `SC-WOW-001` (cosmology hub and triad test), `SC-WOW-DF-001` (Dragonflight), `SC-WOW-CLASS-001` (classes), `SC-WOW-RES-001` (resurrection range) and `SC-WOW-FALL-001` (the guardian's fall). All five are explicit-only: words like "dragon", "paladin", "titan" or "legion" in ordinary text do not route to them.

### What the sort shows
1. The same force appears under different relationships. The Light is held by oath (Paladin, Holy Priest) and was taken by force (the Blood Knights and M'uru). The Void is channelled (Shadow Priest) and consumed (Devourer). Sorting by force hides that; sorting by relationship keeps it.
2. Three classes are consent stories. The Death Knight is a liberated vessel who keeps the power; the Evoker is a created soldier freed from a binding relic; the Warlock reverses possession by binding the demon. Each maps onto SC-CONSENT-001's four conditions differently (see the map's resurrection range and SC-WOW-RES-001).
3. Two classes are defined by their inverse. The Shaman's request is the inverse of the Old Gods' binding of the elemental lords; the Druid's keeping is the inverse of the Druids of the Flame's bargain.
4. The Demon Hunter is the inverse of SC-SCA-CONS-001: it consumes the enemy's power, not its own. The warlock's Classic-era Demonic Sacrifice (sacrificing your own demon) and Life Tap (spending your own health) are the direct cases of CONS-001.
5. Composite (Nephilim) classes: the Evoker carries five flights and the mortal races; the Windrunner sisters split one ranger bundle across three forces (SC-AEP-001).

### Rejected or weak rows
- Discipline's 'Atonement' shares a word with Lev 16 and the doctrine of atonement, but the mechanic (damage dealt heals allies) shares no relational attribute with the Day of Atonement. NAME_BORROWING.
- 'Legion' (Mark 5:9) and the Burning Legion share only the word. NAME_BORROWING; the Death Knight row rests on the freed man, not the name.
- Warrior and Mage have no strong tradition row. Recorded as WEAK rather than forced.

## (b) Cosmology

Layers: CANON (the game, by expansion and patch, via Warcraft Wiki citations), NOVEL (novels and Chronicle, where they add to the game), ANNOUNCED (12.2 Eclipse and The Last Titan, not released on 2026-11-25), TRADITION (scripture by verse), SHAX (t594u), COMPILER. Read under SC-AEP-001: a figure is a bundle of attributes; a resemblance is a candidate, not proof.

### Canon checks
- Six forces in three pairs: Light and Void, Order (arcane) and Disorder (fel), Life (nature) and Death; spirit and the elements sit between them (Chronicle Vol. 1, NOVEL; reflected in the game's cosmology chart).
- The Pantheon of Order (Aman'Thul, Eonar, Khaz'goroth, Norgannon, Golganneth, Aggramar) ordered worlds; Sargeras was their champion. He learned the Void lords meant to corrupt a slumbering world-soul, concluded creation was flawed, and set out to burn it with the Burning Legion (Chronicle Vol. 1, NOVEL). The Pantheon imprisoned him at the Seat of the Pantheon and Illidan chose to stay as his jailer (Antorus, 7.3).
- The Old Gods are the Void's agents on Azeroth: whispers, the Black Empire, the Curse of Flesh on the titan-forged (Chronicle Vol. 1). C'Thun (Ahn'Qiraj, 1.9), Yogg-Saron (Ulduar, 3.1; he had corrupted the Keepers), Y'Shaarj (dead before history; its heart powers Garrosh in Siege of Orgrimmar, 5.4; its last breath made the Sha), N'Zoth (Ny'alotha, 8.3). G'huun was made by the titans' own experiment on Old God blood (Uldir, 8.0).
- The Black Blood after the Old Gods' death: Queen Ansurek accepted Xal'atath's offer, stabbed her mother Neferess, who had refused the Black Empire, and had her ascended by force (Nerub-ar Palace, 11.0). Dying, Ansurek says Xal'atath promised to make her kingdom great; Xal'atath answers that she promised nothing and gave everything.
- Xal'atath, Blade of the Black Empire, was Dimensius's harbinger and wanted to be free of him; she helped seal him into the Dark Heart and kept it (Manaforge Omega, 11.2). She leads the Devouring Host in Midnight (launched 2026-03-02) and is the announced final boss of The Worldcore (12.2 Eclipse, early 2027, ANNOUNCED).
- Death: the Arbiter judged each soul to an afterlife. She was built from Zovaal's anima and sigil after the Eternal Ones stripped him of the office; she was struck dormant by the soul of Argus at the end of Legion, so every soul fell into the Maw; Zovaal destroyed her (Sanctum of Domination, 9.1); the kyrian Pelagos volunteered to become the new Arbiter (9.2).
- Zovaal told Sylvanas the First Ones' design left no one truly free and promised "true freedom"; he wanted a willing partner. Sylvanas turned on him when she saw his real aim, a reality remade with him as its ruler (9.1). He had made Frostmourne and the Helm of Domination through the captive Primus, and all three Lich Kings refused to be his herald.
- The Light is a cosmic force, not a god. The naaru are beings of Light; a naaru that is spent can fall into a void phase (L'ura in the Seat of the Triumvirate, 7.3; freed as Midnight Falls, March on Quel'Danas, 12.0). Xe'ra tried to bind Illidan to the Light's prophecy and he destroyed her (7.3). In Midnight, the Voidspire wing text reads "The Light, once a source of hope and balance, has become a weapon of tyranny" (Lightblinded Vanguard, 12.0).
- The Titans' preservation has a reset: Algalon the Observer arrives to run re-origination on Azeroth if it is judged corrupt (Ulduar, 3.1).
- The Last Titan (ANNOUNCED for 2027): a return to Northrend and Ulduar, the titans' return, and a conspiracy that 'challenges everything previously thought known about the intentions of the titans'. Nothing about it is canon yet.

## (d) Dragonflight as Shax frames it (SC-CONSENT-001 / SC-SCC-001)

Shax (t594u, verbatim): "I even go far as dragonflight as being when the dragon gets to spread their wings for good or and defeat the bad dragon, you know, the good Tok'ra versus the bad ghouls." 'Ghouls' is his word for the Goa'uld (SC-TRIAD-SG-001) and is kept as written. In Warcraft, ghouls are also the Scourge's raised dead, which sit on the possession side of the same split; that is a coincidence of words, noted, not a claim about what he meant.

Layers: CANON (game, by patch), NOVEL (War of the Scaleborn; Chronicle), TRADITION, SHAX, COMPILER.

### What holds of Shax's image
- The dragons do come home and fly free. The dragonflights return to the Dragon Isles after about ten thousand years away, and Dragonflight (10.0) introduced dragonriding. The arc ends with the Aspects restored and a new Aspect, Vyranoth, joining them (10.2).
- Same origin, split by choice. Aspects and Incarnates were all proto-dragons. The Aspects accepted the titans' gift ("Unto each of you is given a gift; unto all of you is given the duty"); the Incarnates refused it and wanted to cleanse the world of the 'stain' of the Pantheon of Order. Tok'ra and Goa'uld are likewise one species split by how they treat hosts (SC-TRIAD-SG-001).
- The Goa'uld case is the Old Gods and Neltharion. He first gave in to the Old Gods' whispers in the War of the Scaleborn, to banish Raszageth and then to beat Iridikron (NOVEL); driven mad, he became Deathwing (killed in Dragon Soul, 4.3). His uncorrupted essence endured under the corruption: a dracthyr evoker recovers it from his last moments to forge Nasz'uro, the Unbound Legacy (10.1). A Goa'uld host is likewise still aware beneath the parasite.
- The bad dragons fall. Raszageth dies freeing her kin (Vault of the Incarnates, 10.0). Sarkareth, the dracthyr who claimed Neltharion's legacy and the Void, dies in Aberrus (10.1). Fyrakk, made crueller by swallowing Neltharion's Shadowflame, is killed in Amirdrassil (10.2). Refusal is self-destruction (RUL-BINARY-001 rev 2).
- The redemption line runs through inheritance without guilt. Wrathion: "I will NOT be held accountable for the sins of my father" (compare Ezek 18:20 and SC-VESSEL-CAIN-001's default). Sabellian comes to see that Neltharion's leadership was flawed even before the corruption and withdraws his claim; he and Wrathion turn to their brother Ebyssian, who becomes the Earth-Warder (10.1).
- The refuser who cooperates is restored. Vyranoth warns Alexstrasza of Fyrakk's plan to burn the World Tree, fights beside the Aspects, and is empowered by Azeroth as Aspect of Storms (10.2).

### Where it breaks
1. The good side broke consent first, by the novel's account. The Aspects were taking proto-dragon eggs from the wild to infuse with Order magic, although Alexstrasza had promised Vyranoth that being ordered would never be forced on anyone; that, with Raszageth's imprisonment, is what turned Vyranoth (War of the Scaleborn, NOVEL).
2. Order magic was used as a Goa'uld tool. Neltharion bound the dracthyr to his will with the Oathbinder, a titan relic; when it broke he sealed them in stasis rather than let them choose (Forbidden Reach, 10.0). The evoker's Nasz'uro is made as the Oathbinder's opposite, to stop the dracthyr from being controlled (10.1).
3. The Primalists are refusers, not parasites. Their crimes are of other kinds: Iridikron used his own clutch-sister Ikronia as a propaganda tool, hundreds died in his failed Incarnate experiments (NOVEL), and Fyrakk bargained with the Druids of the Flame to burn Amirdrassil. They fit the Goa'uld seat only when they join the Void.
4. The 'bad dragon' was not all defeated. Iridikron was never beaten: he drew Galakrond's essence into the Dark Heart (Dawn of the Infinite, 10.1.5), gave it to the Harbinger, Xal'atath, and told Vyranoth they would meet again when the titans face their reckoning (10.2). The Dark Heart carries the arc into The War Within.
5. The split also runs through one dragon over time. Murozond, leader of the infinite dragonflight, is a possible future Nozdormu; in Dawn of the Infinite, Chrono-Lord Deios turns the young Nozdormu into Murozond and Chromie undoes it.
6. The blessing was not returned by the titans. The Aspects lost their titan-given power killing Deathwing (4.3) and regained it from Azeroth herself after Amirdrassil bloomed (10.2). The restored Aspects stand on the world-soul's gift, not the Pantheon's.
7. Wrathion's redemption had a purge in it: after his egg was purified he saw to the death of every other black dragon he knew of (Cataclysm legendary rogue line).

### Tradition (candidates)
- Same root, split by response: the parable of the sower (Matt 13:3-9) is a weak match; the stronger one is Deut 30:19 ("I have set before you life and death ... therefore choose life"). Difference: the gift here comes from a pantheon that a later expansion is announced to question.
- Possession with the self still present: Mark 5:1-15 (the man is "in his right mind" once the spirits leave). Neltharion's essence survives, but he is not restored.
- Inheritance without guilt: Ezek 18:20; Deut 24:16. Tension kept: Exod 20:5.

### Holds
1. The Void is the outside owner. Whispers, the Curse of Flesh, Black Blood ascension and Xal'atath's bargains all take or bend a host's will. Ansurek's line ("promised nothing, gave everything") is the bargain shape of SC-CONSENT-001's failed informed-consent condition.
2. The assimilator comes from the origin layer again (SC-TRIAD-001 break 7). Sargeras was the Pantheon's champion; Zovaal was the first Arbiter; G'huun was made by the titans' experiment.
3. Only cooperation is stable (RUL-BINARY-001 rev 2). Every finale is a coalition (Horde and Alliance, covenants, Aspects, the Light and the Void-touched under Alleria). Each force that refuses cooperation is destroyed or imprisoned: Sargeras (7.3), Zovaal (9.2), the Old Gods one by one.
4. Corrupted creation recurs: G'huun from the titans; Ula'tek, summoned by the Amani as a weapon against the Aqir, proved as much a threat as the enemy (Venomous Abyss, 12.1), the GoT Walkers pattern (SC-GOT-001).

### Breaks
1. The Order side is not clean. Algalon's re-origination is preservation by erasure; the Oathbinder (SC-WOW-DF-001) is Order magic used to bind a will; The Last Titan is announced to question the titans' intentions.
2. The Light coerces too: Xe'ra and Illidan (7.3); the Lightblinded Vanguard (12.0); the Blood Knights drew their Light from a captive naaru, M'uru (TBC 2.4). A triad that puts the Light on the 'good' side by force type fails.
3. The Legion wants annihilation, not hosts. Like the Night King, it fits the triad only on the consent axis.
4. Six forces in three pairs do not reduce to three factions; the triad is a reading of relationships, not a map of the forces.
5. Xal'atath's final motive is not revealed (ANNOUNCED for 12.2); she may yet break the 'outside owner' reading by wanting her own freedom more than any host.

### Tradition (candidates only)
- Fallen guardian: Ezek 28:14-17 (the anointed cherub that covereth, perfect "till iniquity was found in thee") and Isa 14:12-15. Their reading as Satan's fall is interpretive tradition (Origen, Tertullian), not the text's plain subject. See SC-WOW-FALL-001.
- The accuser in the court: Job 1:6-12; Rev 12:10 ("the accuser of our brethren is cast down"). Zovaal was the judge and was cast down. Candidate, with the difference recorded: the satan of Job accuses; Zovaal judged and then rebelled.
- Judgement of souls: 2 Cor 5:10; Rev 20:12. The Arbiter is a built judge, dispassionate by design; scripture's judge is personal. Recorded as a difference.

## (f) The resurrection range

SC-GOT-MTN-001 recorded a range in Game of Thrones: Jon comes back as himself, Beric loses a little each time, the Mountain comes back as a tool. Warcraft has the same range and four kinds the show lacks. Layers: CANON (game by patch), NOVEL, TRADITION, COMPILER. Consent is checked with SC-CONSENT-001's four conditions (voluntary, informed, agency kept, revocable).

### What the range shows
1. The same axis as Game of Thrones: from a restored self (Illidan, like Jon) through a self worn down (Sylvanas, like Beric) to a tool (ghouls, the original Horsemen, like the Mountain).
2. Four kinds the show lacks. The voluntary vessel (Bolvar) takes the binding on himself to hold others. The liberated vessel keeps the binder's power (Death Knights). The liberated become binders (the Ebon Blade raising the new Horsemen; Sylvanas raising new Forsaken): the freed repeat the act they were freed from. The Light inside undeath (Calia; Sir Zeliek, who could still wield the Light in Naxxramas) breaks the rule that undeath and the Light cannot meet.
3. Freedom is mostly given, not taken. The Forsaken were freed by the Lich King's weakness; the Death Knights by his betrayal and Tirion's intervention. Only Bolvar's and Illidan's choices are fully their own.
4. The sentence for Sylvanas is restitution, not death (9.2): the one who raised others without consent is made to return them.

### Tradition (candidates)
- Raising the dead against their will: 1 Sam 28:7-19; Samuel, brought up at Endor, asks "Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up?" (28:15). The prohibition: Deut 18:10-12. Candidate for the Scourge and the Horsemen; difference: Samuel is not enslaved.
- Restoration to the same life: John 11:43-44 (Lazarus). Candidate for Illidan's return; difference: Illidan's return is the work of allies and a jailer, not a word.
- The new body: 1 Cor 15:42-44. No Warcraft case matches; recorded as absent.
- Freed and choosing to serve: Exod 21:5-6. Candidate for the Death Knights under Bolvar and Bolvar himself.
- Restitution: Luke 19:8 (Zacchaeus restores fourfold). Candidate for Sylvanas's sentence.
- A people raised by breath: Ezek 37:1-14. Weak for the Forsaken; the bones are raised whole and by God's spirit, the Forsaken are not.
- The phoenix as a resurrection figure: 1 Clement 25 (early Christian use of the phoenix). Candidate for Belo'ren; tradition, not scripture.

## Appendix: the guardian's fall (SC-WOW-FALL-001)

### The repair: a voluntary successor
Each fallen guardian is followed by one who takes the charge by choice and with a different method: Kalecgos after Malygos (chosen by his flight, he shares magic rather than hoarding it); Bolvar after Arthas ("There must always be a Lich King", 3.3); Pelagos, who volunteered to become the Arbiter after Zovaal (9.2); Ebyssian after Neltharion, chosen by his brothers (10.1); Illidan, who chose to stay as Sargeras's jailer (7.3). The successor's consent is the repair.

### Tradition (candidates)
- Saul: "Because I saw that the people were scattered from me ... I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering" (1 Sam 13:11-12). A king who overrides the rule from fear of the coming threat and loses the kingdom (13:13-14). Attributes 1, 2, 3, 5. CANDIDATE. Successor: David, who refuses to seize power from Saul (1 Sam 24:6).
- Caiaphas: "it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not" (John 11:47-50), from fear the Romans would take the nation (11:48). Attributes 1, 2, 3. CANDIDATE.
- Peter: rebukes the cross to protect his teacher and is told "Get thee behind me, Satan" (Matt 16:21-23); later draws a sword and is told to put it away (John 18:10-11). The protective override refused. CONTRAST.
- The covering cherub: "Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth ... till iniquity was found in thee" (Ezek 28:14-15); with Isa 14:12-15. A guardian's fall. Reading these as Satan's fall is interpretive tradition, not the text's plain subject (the prince of Tyre; the king of Babylon). CANDIDATE with that difference recorded.
- The pattern's difference from Lucifer in tradition: the tradition's fall is pride (1 Tim 3:6); the Warcraft bundle's fall is fear. Recorded, not resolved.
