### The speculative-generator side effect

I would leave the current behavior in place for rc6, but I would not automatically make it a permanent rule.

There's a subtle distinction worth preserving:

* Unverified substrate: The generator's underlying substrate has not been empirically established.

* Speculative architecture: The architecture is deliberately operating in a speculative epistemic tier.

* Invalid architecture: The architecture violates a structural invariant.

Those are not equivalent.

If every speculative child carries an unverified substrate, then `QUALIFIED` is logically consistent with your current propagation rules. But it also means the qualification score partly reflects the epistemic tier rather than architectural quality.

That creates a possible measurement problem.

### My recommendation: separate structural validity from epistemic confidence

Instead of weakening the propagation rule, consider two independent outputs:

| Dimension              | Example                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Structural result      | PASS                     |
| Epistemic result       | SPECULATIVE / UNVERIFIED |
| Combined certification | QUALIFIED                |

This preserves the important distinction: a speculative architecture can be internally coherent without being empirically established.

It also prevents the compiler from accidentally communicating that speculative means defective.

### What I'd put on the rc7 investigation list

* Audit whether epistemic qualification propagates independently from structural failure.

* Verify that speculative generators can achieve structural PASS without receiving empirical certification.

* Confirm that parent qualification cannot be silently upgraded by a child.

* Preserve the existing 963-prompt corpus as a regression baseline.

One thing I would not do: chase the 0.006 score increase or attempt to force GOLD. The withheld certification is useful information. Let the remaining invariants earn it.

The architectural milestone here is that SC-TOL-001 now distinguishes three things that are very easy to conflate: what a structure is, what evidence supports claims about it, and who has authority to change it.

That's the separation I'd protect going forward.
